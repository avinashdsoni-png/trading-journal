What the seven lights mean — in plain words
1 Return on capital Plant one dollar in this business. How many cents grow back every year? Green: more than 15 cents. Below 8 cents the business is quietly eating money. This is the single best test of whether a company is any good.
2 Growth Are sales getting bigger? Green: more than 10% a year. Nothing else can grow for long if this does not. A shrinking top line means the company is losing customers, whatever else the numbers say.
3 Debt If every dollar of operating profit went straight to the bank, how many years to be debt-free? Green: under 2 years. Over 4 years and the bank, not you, decides what happens in a bad year. This is the number that separates the companies that survive a crash from the ones that never come back.
4 Cash conversion The company says it made a profit. Did the money actually turn up in the bank? Green: more than 80 cents of every reported dollar. Below 60 cents, for two years running, usually means the profit exists on paper only. One honest exception: a company spending heavily to build something new will look bad here on purpose — check what the spending is for before judging it.
5 Gross margin Out of every dollar a customer pays, how much is left after making the thing? The level barely matters — software keeps 80 cents, a grocer keeps 25, and both can be fine. Green: the number is holding steady or rising. Falling means competitors are taking the pricing power away, and that shows up here long before it shows up in the profit.
6 Share count You own a slice of a pizza. Is the pizza being cut into more slices? Green: the number of slices is flat or falling. If management keeps cutting new slices, your piece shrinks every year even while the company grows. Quietly, this can eat half your return over a decade.
7 Price — did earnings or mood drive it? A stock goes up for two reasons: the business earned more, or people simply decided to pay more for the same earnings. Green: the earnings did the work. Red means the price outran the profits, so you are paying more per dollar of earnings than a year ago. Not automatically bad — but the rise was mood, and mood can reverse overnight.
Reading the dots. Three states, and the difference matters. A solid dot passed. A dull dot failed. A hollow dot could not be judged at all, because your CSV has no column for it. So 4/4 of 7 does not mean a perfect company — it means four lights passed and three were never tested. Add the missing columns and those hollow dots start giving real answers. Hover any row to see exactly which light did what.

Banks are different. Borrowing is what a bank sells, so lights 3 and 4 are switched off for them automatically. Judge a bank on lights 1, 2, 6 and 7 only.

Light 7 is not the same test IVA Pro runs. On the chart, IVA Pro asks whether the stock is cheaper than its own three-year average — it can see three years of history. Here there is only today's snapshot, so this light asks the closest honest question a snapshot can answer: over the last year, did profits or mood move the price? Use this one to sort the list; use IVA Pro before you buy.

MINERVINI · STAGE & TREND
Ticked names that pass the watchlist filters above. Journal proxies from the screener columns and your upload history; the deep dive measures the real thing. Hover any cell for the formula.
Stage (Minervini ch.5): 2 = price above the 200-day SMA, the 50 above the 200 and the 200 rising; 4 = the mirror; 1 and 3 by the same lines. The 200-day slope compares today’s SMA 200 with the upload ≥ 4 weeks older (“?” while the history is shorter: the 50 above or below the 200 stands in). TT = Trend Template, 2013 version /8 and 2017 version /9 (≥ 25% above the low, RS line rising). RS rank = IBD-style percentile 1–99 inside the name’s Russell run: 0.4 × last 3M + 0.2 × the 3M before + 0.4 × the 6M before that. Mountain = where in Stage 2: EARLY / MID / LATE from % above the 200-day, % above the 52-week low and the weeks the 50 has been above the 200. SEPA: T trend · F fundamentals (EPS ≥ 25% and revenue ≥ 10%, quarterly YoY when the column exists) · C catalyst (your tag catalyst:…) · E entry (within 5% of the high and ≤ 1 ATR from the 20 EMA) · X exit (stop to the 50-day within the ceiling). Category is the journal’s proposal from RS rank inside its industry, size and the EPS trend; set it yourself with a tag cat:leader, cat:competitor, cat:institutional, cat:turnaround, cat:cyclical or cat:laggard on the card. Size = risk % ÷ stop % (ch.13); R:R = Street upside ÷ stop. Needs these screener columns: SMA 50 (1D), SMA 200 (1D), SMA 30 (1W) as the 150-day, EPS diluted growth % Quarterly YoY, Revenue growth % Quarterly YoY.
